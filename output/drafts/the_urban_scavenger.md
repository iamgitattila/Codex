# THE URBAN SCAVENGER
## Finding Food, Materials, and Resources in Abandoned Cities

**Archetype:** Urban Survivor
**Emotional Hook:** "The city didn't disappear. It transformed into the world's largest supply depot. You just need to know where to look."

---

# CHAPTER 1: THE ABANDONED CITY AS RESOURCE
## Why Urban Collapse Creates Opportunity

When most people imagine collapse, they see cities as death traps. Crowded, dependent, resource-depleted. They flee to rural areas seeking safety.

**But cities don't become empty during collapse. They become FULL—full of abandoned resources.**

**Think about what a city contains:**

**Food:** Grocery stores, restaurants, warehouses, vending machines, homes with pantries. Millions of pounds of food.

**Water:** Millions of gallons in water towers, building tanks, swimming pools, water heaters, pipes.

**Tools and equipment:** Hardware stores, construction sites, workshops, garages. Everything from hammers to generators.

**Medicine:** Pharmacies, hospitals, clinics, veterinary offices. Antibiotics, pain medication, surgical supplies.

**Clothing and gear:** Department stores, sporting goods stores, military surplus, homes and apartments.

**Building materials:** Lumber yards, construction sites, demolished buildings. Wood, metal, concrete, insulation.

**Vehicles and parts:** Car dealerships, parking garages, driveways. Functional vehicles (if you have fuel) or parts for repair.

**Fuel:** Gas stations (underground tanks retain fuel even after pumps fail), vehicle tanks, heating oil tanks, propane.

**Electronics and communications:** Cell phones, radios, batteries, solar panels, computers (for parts or data).

**Weapons:** Gun stores, police stations, military bases, homes (US has 400+ million civilian firearms).

**All of this remains after initial looting wave.**

**During first days of collapse, people loot obvious targets (grocery stores, pharmacies, gun stores). But cities are VAST. Looters are disorganized. They miss 70-90% of resources.**

**The skilled scavenger knows what they miss. And how to get it.**

# CHAPTER 2: SCAVENGING STRATEGY AND TARGET SELECTION
## Where to Look and What to Take

**The Looting Timeline**

**Day 1-3:** Panic looting. Grocery stores, pharmacies, gun stores stripped. Crowds fight over obvious resources. Dangerous.

**Day 4-14:** Secondary looting. Hardware stores, sporting goods, department stores hit. Less crowded but still dangerous.

**Day 15-30:** Tertiary looting. Homes and apartments ransacked (starting with wealthy neighborhoods, then spreading). Vehicle fuel siphoned.

**Month 2-6:** Opportunistic scavenging. Abandoned vehicles, industrial sites, less-obvious targets. Competition declines (many people fled, died, or secured resources).

**Month 6+:** Strategic scavenging. Remaining survivors target specialized resources (medicine, spare parts, tools, metals). Scavenging becomes profession.

**Your timing matters.**

**Days 1-14:** Stay hidden. Let chaos pass. You're not competing for stripped grocery stores. You're waiting.

**Weeks 2-4:** Begin scavenging when crowds thin. Target less-obvious locations.

**Months 2+:** Systematic scavenging. Map territory, identify targets, extract resources methodically.

**Non-Obvious High-Value Targets**

**Schools:**

Overlooked by looters (not seen as valuable). But schools contain:
- Kitchen (industrial-size food supplies—canned goods, dry goods)
- Nurse's office (first aid supplies, some medications)
- Maintenance area (tools, cleaning supplies, hardware)
- Athletic equipment (clothing, camping gear, water bottles)
- Classroom supplies (paper, duct tape, rope, scissors, etc.)

**Churches:**

- Kitchens (for church dinners—canned goods, bulk food)
- Office supplies
- Clothing donations
- Sometimes food pantries for poor (significant stored food)

**Industrial warehouses:**

- Manufacturer warehouses full of product (clothing, electronics, hardware)
- Distribution centers (everything from soap to power tools)
- Usually on outskirts (less looting pressure than downtown)

**Office buildings:**

- Vending machines (often overlooked—some have tools to open)
- First aid stations
- Break rooms (food, coffee, utensils)
- Janitor closets (cleaning supplies, tools, extension cords)
- IT rooms (servers, computers, batteries, cables)

**Auto repair shops:**

- Tools (often missed because not consumer-facing)
- Oil, fluids, parts
- Battery chargers, welding equipment

**Veterinary clinics:**

- Antibiotics (many are same as human antibiotics—amoxicillin, cephalexin)
- Surgical supplies
- Syringes, needles
- Sutures
- Anesthetics (some veterinary pain meds work for humans)

**Garden centers and nurseries:**

- Seeds (vegetable and herb seeds for planting)
- Fertilizer, soil, pots
- Tools (shovels, hoes, pruners)
- Often in suburban/rural areas (less looted)

**Libraries:**

- Books (knowledge—medical texts, survival guides, repair manuals)
- Quiet, climate-controlled space (potential shelter)
- Community bulletin boards (may have useful local information)

**Dental offices:**

- Pain medications
- Local anesthetics
- Antibiotics
- Sterile instruments
- X-ray equipment (lead shielding = radiation protection material)

**These targets are systematically ignored during initial looting. Weeks later, they're intact.**

**Residential Scavenging**

**Homes and apartments eventually get looted. But most looters are lazy.**

**They check obvious places:**
- Kitchen pantries (food)
- Bedrooms (valuables, guns)
- Garages (tools)

**They miss:**

**Attics:** Holiday decorations, stored clothing, keepsakes. Less valuable but sometimes tools, camping gear, or forgotten supplies.

**Basements:** Workbenches, stored food (canned goods in basement shelves), water heaters (40-80 gallons water), furnace (repairable for heating), building materials.

**Crawl spaces:** Storage, sometimes utilities or hidden valuables.

**False bottoms/hidden safes:** Safes hidden in floors, walls, or closets. Requires time to find and open but may contain guns, cash, gold, documents.

**Sheds and detached structures:** Tools, gardening supplies, chemicals (useful or dangerous depending), fuel cans.

**Vehicles in garages/driveways:** Fuel in tank (5-20 gallons), batteries, tools in trunk, jumper cables, first aid kit.

**When scavenging residences, be thorough. Most scavengers rush. You take time.**

**Food Scavenging**

**After stores are stripped, where is food?**

**Homes:** Average American household has 1-3 weeks food. 100 homes = 100-300 weeks of food total. Canned goods, dry goods, frozen food (spoiled, but canned/dry still good).

**Restaurants (kitchens and storage):** Huge quantities of canned goods, dry goods, spices, oils. Walk-in freezers (food spoiled but some canned/dry goods in storage).

**Vending machines:** Often overlooked after initial looting. Contain snacks with long shelf life (chips, candy, crackers). Break into them (crowbar or smash front).

**Food banks/pantries:** May be cleaned out, but rural/church-based food banks are sometimes missed.

**Agricultural supply stores:** Animal feed (grain, corn, oats). Technically for livestock but humans can eat it. Not appetizing, but calories.

**Abandoned gardens:** Vegetables still growing in yards of evacuated/dead residents. Free food.

**Fruit trees:** Urban and suburban areas have fruit trees (apples, pears, peaches, citrus). Many go unharvested normally. During collapse, these are free food source.

**Dumpsters behind grocery stores:** Food thrown out before collapse (expired or damaged but still edible). In first weeks, dumpsters still contain edible food before fully spoiled.

**Food supply in cities is distributed, not centralized. Thorough scavenger finds food for months.**

# CHAPTER 3: SCAVENGING SAFETY, GEAR, AND LONG-TERM STRATEGY
## Surviving While You Search

**Scavenging Safety Rules**

**Rule 1: Assume every building is occupied.**

Survivors hide in abandoned buildings. Some are friendly. Some are hostile. Some are territorial and will ambush scavengers.

**Before entering:** Observe. Watch for signs of occupation (smoke from chimney, covered windows, movement, defensive preparations). Listen (voices, generators, dogs barking).

**Announce yourself (if you choose to engage):** "Hello, I'm just looking for supplies. I don't want trouble." Some survivors appreciate warning. Others see it as weakness. Judgment call.

**Or enter silently and leave if you encounter occupants.** Say "Sorry, didn't know anyone was here" and exit non-threateningly.

**Never assume empty building is safe.**

**Rule 2: Watch for booby traps.**

Paranoid survivors trap their caches or shelters. Tripwires, deadfalls, shotgun traps (illegal but used), bells/alarms.

**Check entryways:** Look for wires across doorways, pressure plates, suspicious strings.

**Move slowly:** Don't rush. Rushing triggers traps.

**Rule 3: Structural safety.**

Abandoned buildings decay. Roofs collapse, floors rot, stairs break.

**Test floors before walking:** Tap with stick or stomp gently before committing weight.

**Avoid upper floors in damaged buildings:** Too easy to fall through.

**Watch for fire damage:** Burned buildings are structurally compromised.

**Rule 4: Disease and contamination.**

Dead bodies create disease risk. Rodent droppings (hantavirus), bat guano (histoplasmosis), mold, chemical spills.

**Wear mask (N95 or better) in buildings with death smell or heavy mold.**

**Don't disturb rodent nests or droppings.**

**Avoid confined spaces with dead bodies (infection risk).**

**Rule 5: Bring backup (or don't).**

**Partner scavenging:** Safer (two people watch each other's backs). Efficient (carry more). But splits resources.

**Solo scavenging:** Riskier (no backup if injured or attacked). Slower (one person carries less). But you keep everything.

**Trade-off depends on trust, available partners, and risk level.**

**Scavenging Gear**

**Carry:**

**Backpack or bag:** Carry scavenged items. Larger is better (hiking backpack, duffle bag, shopping cart if you're bold).

**Crowbar:** Open doors, pry open crates, break locks. Multi-use tool.

**Flashlight/headlamp:** Dark buildings. Headlamp is hands-free (better than flashlight).

**Gloves:** Protect hands from sharp edges, broken glass, rusty metal, contamination.

**Mask:** N95 or respirator. Dust, mold, smoke, disease protection.

**Knife:** Defense, utility (cutting, prying, opening packages).

**Water bottle:** Stay hydrated during long scavenging runs.

**Rope or cord:** Hauling gear, securing loads, climbing.

**First aid kit:** Treat cuts, scrapes, injuries immediately.

**Weapon:** Handgun, knife, bat, or improvised weapon. Scavengers are targets for robbery.

**Map and marker:** Track locations you've scavenged (avoid revisiting empty sites).

**Wear:**

**Sturdy boots:** Broken glass, nails, debris everywhere. Boots protect feet.

**Long sleeves and pants:** Protection from cuts, scrapes, sun, insects.

**Neutral/drab colors:** Don't stand out. Urban camouflage (grays, browns, blacks) helps you blend.

**Avoid:**

**Bright colors:** Make you visible/target.

**Excessive weight:** Scavenging is exhausting. Pack light, make multiple trips if needed.

**Loud/clinking gear:** Stealth matters. Wrap metal tools in cloth to reduce noise.

**Long-Term Scavenging Strategy**

**Phase 1: Survival (Weeks 1-4)**

Goal: Secure immediate needs (food, water, medicine, weapons, shelter supplies).

**Targets:** Stores, homes, offices (prioritizing nearby locations).

**Risk tolerance:** Low (avoid conflict, avoid crowds, avoid obvious danger).

**Phase 2: Establishment (Months 2-6)**

Goal: Build stockpile, secure tools/equipment for long-term survival, identify renewable resources.

**Targets:** Warehouses, specialty stores, industrial sites, agricultural supplies.

**Risk tolerance:** Moderate (travel farther, take more time per site, occasional calculated risk).

**Phase 3: Specialization (Month 6+)**

Goal: Acquire specialized knowledge, rare items, trade goods.

**Targets:** Libraries (books), medical facilities (advanced equipment), machine shops (tools), electronics (parts for repair/comms).

**Risk tolerance:** Variable (depends on competition and security situation).

**Mapping and Documentation**

**Keep records:**

**Scavenging map:** Mark buildings you've searched. Rate them (stripped, partially looted, intact, dangerous). This prevents wasting time revisiting empty sites.

**Resource locations:** Note high-value finds (e.g., "Warehouse on 5th St has 500 lbs rice," "Auto shop on Elm has welding gear").

**Danger zones:** Mark hostile territories, gang-controlled areas, structurally unsound buildings, contaminated sites.

**Use paper map or mental map (digital devices might not work).** Physical notes hidden in secure location.

**Scavenging Ethics and Community Relations**

**Taking from dead/abandoned = morally neutral.** They don't need it. You do.

**Taking from living/occupied = theft.** Creates conflict. Avoid unless desperate or you're willing to fight.

**Trading scavenged goods:** Scavenging becomes profession. Find items others need, trade for items you need. Scavenger becomes merchant.

**Community scavenging teams:** In established survivor communities, organized scavenging teams go out systematically, bring resources back to community, distribute fairly. This is more efficient and safer than solo scavenging.

**Joining or forming such team gives you protection, labor division, and community support.**

**The Scavenger's Mindset**

**See opportunity, not disaster.**

**Dead city = storage facility.**

**Collapsed civilization = inventory waiting to be claimed.**

**Every building is treasure chest. Most people walked past it. You open it.**

**The scavenger who survives is:**

**Thorough:** Checks everywhere, not just obvious places.

**Patient:** Doesn't rush into danger. Observes, plans, executes carefully.

**Knowledgeable:** Knows what's valuable (medicine, tools, seeds, ammo, rare skills/books).

**Adaptable:** Changes strategy based on conditions (competition, danger, resources).

**Disciplined:** Takes only what's needed/valuable. Doesn't hoard junk.

**Urban collapse is supply chain collapse. But the supplies didn't disappear. They're just sitting in different warehouses—abandoned warehouses called buildings.**

**You're not looting. You're reclaiming resources from the ruins.**

**The city is still full. You just need to know where to look.**
