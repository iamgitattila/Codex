# THE TUNNEL RATS
## Underground Movement Through Sewers, Subways, and Utility Tunnels

**Archetype:** Urban Survivor
**Emotional Hook:** "When every street is a killzone, when checkpoints control movement, when going outside means death—you go underground."

---

# CHAPTER 1: THE HIDDEN CITY BENEATH YOUR FEET
## Why Underground Matters

Every city is built on infrastructure hidden beneath streets.

**Storm sewers** drain rainwater.
**Sanitary sewers** carry waste.
**Subway tunnels** (in major cities) transport trains.
**Utility tunnels** house water pipes, electrical lines, steam pipes, communications cables.
**Service tunnels** connect basements of major buildings.
**Abandoned infrastructure** (old subway lines, closed stations, forgotten maintenance passages).

This creates a parallel city underground—miles of interconnected passages allowing movement without ever surfacing.

**Most people never think about it. Urban explorers know it exists. And those who've survived urban warfare know it can save your life.**

**Why Underground Movement Matters in Urban Collapse**

**Surface streets become death zones:**

**Checkpoints:** Military or gang-controlled checkpoints restrict movement. Papers checked, belongings searched, bribes extracted, or entry denied/arrested.

**Snipers:** In urban warfare (Sarajevo, Syria, Mogadishu), snipers control key streets. Crossing certain intersections = death. Locals learn which streets are "sniper alleys" and avoid them.

**Artillery/bombing:** Buildings on surface are targets. Shells, rockets, bombs rain down randomly or targeted.

**Surveillance:** Drones, cameras, patrols. Moving on surface = being tracked.

**Gangs/warlords:** Rival factions control territories. Wrong person in wrong neighborhood = kidnapping, robbery, or murder.

**In this environment, those who control underground movement control logistics, escape routes, and tactical advantage.**

**Historical Examples**

**Cu Chi Tunnels (Vietnam War):** Viet Cong built extensive tunnel networks beneath Cu Chi district. 250 kilometers of tunnels connecting villages, hiding supplies, and enabling surprise attacks. Americans called VC fighters who used tunnels "tunnel rats." Despite overwhelming US firepower, tunnels made VC nearly impossible to defeat.

**Sarajevo Siege (1992-1996):** Serbian forces surrounded Sarajevo, Bosnia. Snipers controlled surface streets. Civilians dug tunnel under airport runway connecting besieged city to outside territory. 800-meter tunnel allowed smuggling of food, weapons, and people. Kept city alive.

**Gaza Tunnels:** Extensive tunnel networks under Gaza-Egypt border allow smuggling despite blockade. Hamas uses tunnels for military operations. Tunnels are regularly bombed/flooded but constantly rebuilt. Tunnels mean survival and resistance.

**Manila Sewer Guerrillas (WWII):** Filipino resistance fighters used Manila's sewer system to move supplies, conduct sabotage, and escape Japanese patrols.

**Paris Catacombs (WWII):** French Resistance used catacombs (underground ossuaries) and abandoned quarries for meetings, hiding weapons, and evading Nazis.

**Pattern: Urban warfare makes surface movement suicidal. Those who master underground movement survive and resist.**

**What You Can Access Underground**

**Depends on city size and age.**

**Major cities (New York, Chicago, Los Angeles, Boston, etc.):**

Extensive sewer systems (combined or separate storm/sanitary), subway tunnels, utility tunnels, service passages, steam tunnels (older cities with central heating), abandoned infrastructure.

**Access is theoretically possible but highly restricted, dangerous, and illegal.**

**Medium cities:**

Storm sewers, sanitary sewers (usually separate), limited utility tunnels, basements of connected buildings.

**Small towns:**

Limited underground infrastructure. Maybe culverts under roads, small storm drains. Less useful for movement.

**This chapter focuses on what's realistic to access and use in collapse scenarios when law enforcement isn't functioning.**

# CHAPTER 2: SEWER AND STORM DRAIN NAVIGATION
## Accessing and Moving Through Underground Drains

**Storm Drains vs. Sanitary Sewers**

**Storm drains:** Carry rainwater from streets to rivers/ocean. Enter through grates on street, outlet pipes on hillsides, or culverts under roads. USUALLY don't carry human waste (though some older cities have combined systems).

**Sanitary sewers:** Carry toilet waste and wastewater from buildings. Connect to treatment plants. ALWAYS contain human waste, bacteria, and toxic gases.

**For underground movement, storm drains are vastly preferable (cleaner, safer, less toxic gas).**

**Finding Storm Drain Access**

**Surface clues:**

**Street grates:** Metal grates on curbs where water drains during rain. These lead to catch basins (small underground chambers) which connect to larger storm drain pipes.

**Outlet pipes:** Look for concrete or metal pipes exiting hillsides, cliffs, or creek banks. These are outlets where storm drains empty. Can be entered from outside (often easier than entering from street level).

**Culverts:** Large pipes or tunnels that carry streams under roads. Storm drains often connect to these.

**Manhole covers:** Some labeled "STORM" or "SD" (storm drain). Others are sanitary sewer (labeled "SEWER" or "SS"). Some are unlabeled. Wrong guess = entering sanitary sewer.

**Mapping storm drains:**

**Before collapse:** Research. Some urban explorer communities share maps. Some cities have infrastructure maps (officially restricted but occasionally leaked). Google Earth shows many outlet pipes.

**Physical exploration:** Follow storm drain system. Enter via outlet, explore upstream. Take notes, draw map, photograph junctions. Build mental model of system.

**GPS and mapping tools:** Mark entrances and junctions. In collapse, GPS might not work, but you'll have mental map + notes.

**Entering Storm Drains**

**Street grates:** Most are too small for adult human. Kids can fit. Some larger grates are removable (crowbar lifts grate).

**Manholes:** Need manhole key (hook-shaped tool that lifts cover). Improvise with crowbar or large screwdriver. Manhole covers are heavy (100+ lbs). Two people better than one.

**Outlet pipes:** Often easiest access. Hike to outlet location (usually creek, river, or drainage canal). Enter pipe. Low-profile at outlet (you're visible to anyone nearby). Move inside quickly.

**Safety considerations:**

**Never enter during rain or storms.** Flash flooding in storm drains kills quickly. Water rises from ankle-deep to chest-deep in seconds. You'll be swept away and drown.

**Check weather forecast.** If rain is predicted within next 24 hours, don't enter. Even distant rain can cause flash flooding in drains miles away.

**Toxic gases:** Less common in storm drains than sanitary sewers, but still possible. Methane (flammable), hydrogen sulfide (toxic, smells like rotten eggs, heavier than air). Carry gas detector if possible. If you smell strong chemical or rotten egg smell, exit immediately.

**Rats and wildlife:** Rats live in drains. Bites can transmit disease. Snakes, raccoons, opossums also use drains. Mostly harmless but startling in dark.

**Getting lost:** Storm drain systems are mazes. Without map/memory, easy to get disoriented. Mark your path (chalk marks, reflective tape, counting junctions).

**Getting stuck:** Pipes get smaller. If you enter tight pipe and it narrows further, you might not be able to back out. Test pipes before committing. Send smallest person first as scout.

**Navigating Underground**

**Lighting:**

**Headlamp:** Essential. Hands-free lighting. Bring spare batteries or rechargeable with solar charger.

**Flashlight:** Backup. If headlamp fails, you're blind underground.

**Glow sticks:** Emergency lighting if batteries die. Last 8-12 hours. Cheap, lightweight, waterproof.

**Movement techniques:**

**Crouch-walking:** Many storm drains are 4-6 feet tall. Can't stand upright. Crouch-walk or duck-walk. Exhausting but necessary.

**Crawling:** Small pipes (3-4 feet diameter) require hands-and-knees crawling. Kneepads help.

**Wading:** Water flows through storm drains (especially after rain). Ankle to knee-deep water is common. Waterproof boots or accept wet feet.

**Climbing:** Some sections have vertical drops or climbs. Ladders on walls (rusty, may be unsafe). Rope useful for lowering packs or assisting others.

**Orientation:**

**Water flows downstream.** If lost, follow water flow to outlet (river/ocean). This leads you OUT eventually.

**Junctions and intersections:** Storm drains branch. Every junction is decision point. Mark path so you can retrace.

**Compass bearings:** Compass works underground (unless near large metal masses). Know which direction you're trying to go.

**Equipment for Underground Movement**

**Clothing:**

**Waterproof boots or waders:** Feet will be wet. Waterproof = warmer and fewer infections.

**Layers:** Underground is cool (60-70°F year-round in most climates). Dress warmly but in layers you can remove if exertion heats you up.

**Gloves:** Protect hands from sharp edges, rust, filth.

**Hat:** Protects head from low ceilings (you will hit your head repeatedly).

**Gear:**

**Headlamp + spare batteries**

**Rope (50-100 feet):** For climbing, lowering packs, assisting others.

**Chalk or spray paint:** Mark your route.

**First aid kit:** Cuts and scrapes are inevitable.

**Water and snacks:** Even short underground trips are exhausting.

**Radio or communication:** If traveling with group, walkie-talkies help coordinate.

**Weapons:** Underground is close-quarters. Knife, club, or handgun. Long guns are awkward in tight spaces.

**Respirator or dust mask:** For toxic gases or dust (some sections are very dry and dusty).

# CHAPTER 3: SUBWAY TUNNELS, BASEMENTS, AND TACTICAL UNDERGROUND MOVEMENT
## Advanced Urban Underground Skills

**Subway Tunnel Movement**

**Major cities with subways:** New York, Boston, Philadelphia, Washington DC, Chicago, San Francisco Bay Area, Los Angeles, Atlanta, Miami.

**Subway tunnels offer advantages:**

**Height:** Tall enough to walk upright. Much easier than storm drains.

**Length:** Can travel miles underground.

**Strategic locations:** Subways connect key areas (downtown, transit hubs, neighborhoods).

**But also serious dangers:**

**Live rails:** Electrified third rail carries 600-750 volts DC. Touch it = instant death. Even in collapse, some sections might retain power (if any part of grid functions).

**Trains:** If subway is still operating (even partially), trains can appear suddenly. Outrunning train in tunnel = unlikely survival.

**Security:** Subways are heavily surveilled and patrolled (pre-collapse). Post-collapse, might be gang-controlled or used as shelter by refugees (hostile encounters).

**Dead-ends:** Subway tunnels are LINEAR. Limited exits. If trapped between hostile forces, you have few escape options.

**Accessing subways:**

**Station entrances:** Most obvious. Walk down to platform, enter tunnel. But station platforms are public spaces (easily observed/controlled).

**Ventilation grates:** Large grates on sidewalks vent subway tunnels. Some can be opened. Drop into tunnel between stations (more covert entry).

**Emergency exits:** Subways have emergency exits to surface at intervals. Often locked from inside (preventing public entry) but might be forced or unlocked during collapse.

**Connecting tunnels:** Some utility tunnels and service passages connect to subway system.

**Navigating subway tunnels:**

**Stay off live rail:** Third rail is mounted beside or between tracks. Usually has protective cover (yellow or white) but not always. NEVER touch. NEVER step over. Walk on opposite side of tunnel.

**Watch for trains:** Listen constantly. Feel vibration in rails. If train approaches, find alcove (emergency refuge areas built into tunnel walls every 200-300 feet) and press flat against wall.

**Know exits:** Every station is exit. But some stations might be collapsed, blocked, or hostile-controlled. Have alternate exits planned.

**Avoid confrontation:** Tunnels are occupied by homeless, refugees, gangs (depending on collapse stage). Conflict in tunnel is nightmare—close quarters, limited escape, echoing acoustics make communication/coordination impossible.

**Basement and Utility Tunnel Networks**

**In downtown areas of major cities, many buildings have connected basements or utility tunnels.**

**Examples:**

**Chicago:** Extensive freight tunnel system (originally for coal delivery) connects many downtown buildings. Now disused but still accessible.

**New York:** Steam tunnel networks and building service tunnels connect hospitals, universities, and downtown buildings.

**Boston:** Downtown buildings share utility corridors.

**University campuses:** Almost always have utility tunnels connecting buildings (for steam heat, electrical, communications). Often accessible via unlocked doors or maintenance access.

**These networks allow moving between buildings without surfacing.**

**Accessing basements:**

**Building entry:** Enter building, find stairs to basement. In collapse, building security is gone. Many buildings have unsecured basement entrances (loading docks, service doors).

**Tunnel connections:** Once in one basement, explore for doors/passages leading to adjacent buildings. Often labeled "TUNNEL," "MECHANICAL," or "SERVICE."

**Elevator shafts:** Dangerous but possible. Elevator shafts sometimes connect to basements and sub-basements. Climbing down shaft is risky (falling danger, elevator might move).

**Tactical Uses of Underground Movement**

**Escape:** Surrounded by hostile forces on surface. Retreat to basement, access storm drain or tunnel, move underground to exit blocks away.

**Supply transport:** Moving supplies across hostile territory on surface = ambush risk. Move underground = covert. Load supplies at Point A, carry through tunnels, emerge at Point B.

**Cache placement:** Hide supplies in underground locations. Storm drains, tunnels, abandoned subway sections. Difficult for others to find. You know location, can retrieve when needed.

**Observation:** Sewer grates, ventilation shafts, and basement windows allow observing surface from concealment underground.

**Ambush:** Emerge from underground, attack, retreat back underground. Enemy can't pursue (they don't know underground routes).

**Communication:** Underground routes allow messengers to travel between neighborhoods/factions without passing through enemy checkpoints.

**Shelter:** Some underground spaces (large chambers, abandoned subway stations) can serve as temporary shelters. Defensible, concealed, stable temperature.

**Underground Defensive Positions**

**Defending underground positions:**

**Choke points:** Tunnels and passages are natural choke points. Small force can hold tunnel against larger force. Defender can fight one attacker at a time (tunnel is only wide enough for one).

**Darkness:** Defender knows terrain, has lighting. Attacker is blind. HUGE advantage.

**Booby traps:** Wires, tripwires, false passages, flooded sections. These delay and damage attackers.

**Escape routes:** Defender needs multiple exits. If one is cut off, escape via alternate route.

**Disadvantages of underground defense:**

**Smoke/gas:** Attacker can pump smoke or tear gas into tunnel. Defender is flushed out or suffocates.

**Flooding:** Attacker can flood tunnel (divert storm drain, open fire hydrant, break water main upstream). Defender drowns or forced to exit.

**Explosives:** Attacker can collapse tunnel with explosives. Defender is buried.

**Underground positions are strong for ambush and short-term defense but can become traps if attacker is determined and resourceful.**

**Risks and Realities**

**Underground movement is dangerous.**

**Drowning:** Flash floods kill quickly.

**Toxic gases:** Asphyxiation or poisoning.

**Getting lost:** Die of dehydration/starvation in maze of tunnels.

**Injury:** Falls, cuts, broken bones in darkness.

**Disease:** Contact with sewage causes infections (leptospirosis, E. coli, hepatitis).

**Structural collapse:** Old infrastructure might be unstable. Collapse traps or kills.

**But in urban warfare environment where surface movement = death, underground movement is risk worth taking.**

**The tunnel rats who survived Vietnam weren't fearless. They were realistic about risk. They knew tunnel was dangerous. But it was LESS dangerous than surface.**

**Same calculation applies in collapsed urban environment.**

**Master underground movement. Map the tunnels. Practice navigation. Build equipment cache. Establish escape routes.**

**When the city above burns, the city below might save you.**
